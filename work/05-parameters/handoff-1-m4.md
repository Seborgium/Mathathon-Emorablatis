## 1. §2.2 / §7.3 — replace the sentence that justifies `N = 112`

**Delete** (in §7.3): *"The former bound is below the signed 32-bit limit."*
**Insert** in §2.2, immediately after (2.6):

> We take `N = 112`, `w = 1/N`. The choice is constrained at both ends and is not otherwise
> special. At the lower end, a retained bin midpoint covers its whole bin, because a bin is a cube
> of side `1/N` in nine coordinates and `√9/(2N) = 3/(2N) ≤ .032 = R` holds exactly when
> `N ≥ 47`; at `N = 47` the fresh-centre radius is `.031915` and at `N = 112` it is `.013393`,
> i.e. 42% of `R`, so the bin ball is never the binding scale for `R`. At the upper end, the
> five-coordinate bin differences are stored in `int8_t`, which requires `N − 1 ≤ 127`, i.e.
> `N ≤ 128`; the tuple counter `C(N+4,5)` is a signed 32-bit integer, which would allow `N ≤ 189`
> (`C(193,5) = 2,118,000,528 < 2^31`), so the 32-bit limit is 61 values weaker than the storage
> limit and does not determine the choice. Between the two, the reuse test of §2.4 certifies a
> previous centre at integer offset `k` only when `‖k‖² + 9/4 + Σ|k_j|` falls below `(NR)²`, a
> budget that a nonzero `k` can meet only above `4.25`; with `NR = 3.584` and `(NR)² = 12.845`
> the offsets of norm at most `3.58` number `284,055`, while at `N = 56` the budget is `3.21` and
> no nonzero offset can pass, so the mechanism is switched off near the bottom of the window. The cost of the choice is the enumeration
> `C(N+4,5) ≈ N⁵/120`: `75,287,520` at `N = 96`, `160,389,488` at `N = 112`, `309,319,296` at
> `N = 128`, so one grid step of 16 costs about a factor two. We take `N = 112` as a cost choice
> near the top of the admissible band, with 16 units of `int8` headroom; any
> `N ∈ [65, 128]` would leave the argument of §2 unchanged.

And in §7.5, next to Table 1:

> `N = 112` enters the execution only through the tuple counts and the `int8` bin differences; a
> run at any `N ∈ [65,128]` would establish the same statement at a different cost.

**Why this is better than what is there.** The current sentence names a bound that permits 189 and
is not the operative one. The replacement names two inequalities, one at each end, gives the slack
at both, and says plainly that the middle of the window is a cost choice. It is also falsifiable:
a reader can recompute `C(193,5)`, `3/(2·47)` and `127`.

## 2. Def 4.1 / §5.1 / §6.1 — replace `0 ≤ k ≤ 10`

**Delete** `0 ≤ k ≤ 10` in the three places and the phrase "at most ten tangent columns" in
Prop 4.6; **insert** after the range display in §5.1:

> The tangent dimension `k` is bounded in practice by the box cap, not by an independent
> allowance. With `L = ⌈E/w + .500001⌉`, `E = √(1+ort)·R + 1e−7` and `(2L+1)^k ≤ 5·10^7`:
> the mid pass receives radii up to `.126` and gives `L = 11`, `23` boxes per axis, hence
> `k ≤ 5` (`23^5 = 6,436,343`; `23^6 = 148,035,889`); the fine pass receives radius `.016`
> (the batch replacement of §7.2) and gives `L = 4`, `9` boxes per axis, hence `k ≤ 8`; the
> fallback pass gives `L = 5`, `11` boxes per axis, hence `k ≤ 7`. Since every coarse record
> must survive the mid pass, the operative bound is `k ≤ 5`, and a record with `k = 6` cannot be
> refined there. We therefore state `0 ≤ k ≤ 5` and record the observed distribution of `k` over
> the 3,083 coarse and 22,379 fine records.

If instead the authors want to keep `k ≤ 10`, they must raise the box cap to `23^10 = 4.1·10^13`,
which is not a memory model anyone should adopt.

## 3. §6.3 — replace the clustering cutoff (F2)

**Delete** the sentence containing `(.042 − 1.11w)^2`.
**Insert**:

> With `w = 1/M` the candidate threshold is `max(0, .042 − 1.11w)^2`, and the coordinate cutoff
> is the square root of twice that threshold plus `10^-10`. The `max` is not cosmetic: by
> Lemma 6.1, `X_M = √(29/6)/(2M) = 1.0992/M`, so `1.11w` is an upper bound for `X_M` and
> `.042 − 1.11w` is meant to be the margin left after paying the bin norm. That margin is
> negative for `M ≤ 26`, so the printed formula squares a negative number and turns into an
> absolute value: its effective merge radius is `.0505, .0042, .0189, .0304` at
> `M = 12, 24, 48, 96`, which is not monotone in the grid, and at `M = 24` the coordinate cutoff
> `.0060` is below the midpoint spacing `1/24 = .0417`, so no bin can merge into a neighbour at
> all. Either the `max` above, or no cutoff at all, restores monotonicity. In both cases the
> covering property is unchanged: the assigned radius of Lemma 6.4 is recomputed from the final
> assignment, so these constants affect only the quality of the assignment, never its validity.

**Which reading the paper supports.** §6.3 already says "These constants affect only the choice of
assignments; the proof is the final radius update in Lemma 6.4." So the paper's own position is
that the formula is harmless. It is: the defect is non-monotone behaviour and wasted work, not
unsoundness. The `max` is the minimal repair and it changes nothing for `M ≥ 48`, i.e. nothing for
two of the four grids and nothing for the 96 grid where the run does most of its work.

## 4. §4.6 — the `100` sentinel (F4)

**Insert** after "Its failure sentinel is 10":

> The value `100` returned by `ell` on a scale failure is not distinguishable from a certified
> bound: A.8 shows that the certified spectral bound satisfies `gu ≤ 100.1`. What prevents the
> misreading is only the conversion to cycles — `100/(2π) = 15.92`, against the `t < 10` guard —
> a factor `1.59` and nothing more, so any retuning of that neighbourhood (a `t < 20` guard, or a
> sentinel of 5) would silently accept a failure as a certificate. The same value-overloading
> appears in `bootstrap` and `relationB::eval`, where the sentinel `10` is 26 times the largest
> legitimate quantity (A.9 bounds every stored distance by `.378`, and radii by `.126`). A
> non-representable sentinel, e.g. NaN with a checked `isnan`, or a separate status flag, removes
> the hazard at no cost.

## 5. Lemma 4.8 — `q_d < 3.8` contradicts the sentence that uses it

**Delete** "In particular no individual chord reaches `√2`" **or** replace `3.8` by `2`.
Suggested replacement of both:

> Since `Σ_j |D_j′ − D_j°|² ≤ q_d`, every individual chord satisfies `c_j ≤ √q_d`, and the
> derivative bound requires `q_d < 4`; we impose the stronger `q_d < 2`, which also makes
> `|d_j| ≤ π/2` and keeps the arc bound within a factor `√2` of optimal. The value `3.8` printed
> in Lemma 4.8 admits chords up to `1.949`, so the parenthetical claim of the lemma is false
> there; `2` is the largest round value for which it is true, and the source's own `Candidate`
> path never reports a `q_d` above `2` in the recorded run.

**If the authors prefer to keep 3.8**, then the sentence about `√2` must go, and the lemma should
say only what `3.8` supports — that `(1 − q_d/4)^{-1/2}` exists. Either way the honest statement
is cheaper than the current one.

## 6. Lemma 4.11 — justify `β = .45` and `.8`, or replace them

**Insert** after (4.33):

> The two values `β = .45` and `β = .8` are not independent constants: for `u = r²‖A‖²` and
> `v = q²‖B‖²` the bound is `(1+β)u + (1+1/β)v`, minimised at `β = √(v/u)`. The value `.45`
> corresponds to `v/u = .2025` and `.8` to `v/u = .64`, i.e. to an assumption about the relative
> size of the `D`-contribution and the seed contribution. Since any `β > 0` gives a valid
> ellipsoid, a misjudged `β` costs only an inflated bound (at `v/u = 1` the optimal `β = 1` gives
> `4u`, while `.45` gives `4.67u`); we therefore record `u`, `v` and the `β` used with each Record.

## 7. End of Appendix A — print the ratios, and say where the risk is

**Insert** immediately after the closing table:

> The table gives the two columns that matter for soundness and omits the one that matters for
> risk. The ratios margin/error are: `1.25` (external tangent, `.00015` vs `.00012` rad), `10`
> (A flat norm), `25` (A directional), `30` (C joint support), `40` (Root identity), `50`
> (B cross-Gram, Candidate direct support), `100` (five paths), `200` (three paths), then
> `333, 1000, 1000, 2000, 2857, 10^4, 10^4, 10^5, 10^6`. The median is `200`; the minimum is
> `1.25`. Because each margin is compared against one established error and no margin is
> accumulated across stages — a failed comparison produces an unresolved region, never an
> exclusion — the numerical soundness of the whole argument is the *minimum* of these ratios, not
> their product. It is therefore concentrated in a single inequality: if the external tangent
> error were underestimated by 20 per cent, the argument would fail at the bootstrap, not at the
> seed cover. Stating that plainly is worth more to a reader than a table with six-digit ratios.
