# $N=112$: what the bin count controls, what constrains it, and what it costs

Scripts: `experiments/n112.py` (constraints, cost curve), `experiments/seedcover.py` (cover counts),
`experiments/caps.py` (the caps that interact with $N$). Tables: `data/m5-cost-curve-N.csv`,
`data/m5-seedcover-counts.csv`.

## 1. What $N$ controls

$N$ is the resolution of the only *exhaustive* enumeration in the proof. §2.2 puts one closed bin of
width $1/N$ on each axis of the nine seed phases
$q=(x_1,\dots,x_5,y_2,\dots,y_5)\in[0,1]^9$, and Prop. 2.10 concludes that radius-$0.032$ balls
about the *retained* bin midpoints cover the seed coordinates of every exact complex Hadamard
matrix. Three quantities depend on $N$ and nothing else in the proof does in a binding way:

* the **enumeration size**, $C(N+4,5)$ X-tuples plus $N\cdot C(N+3,4)$ Y-tail tests;
* the **bin circumradius** $\sqrt9/(2N)=3/(2N)$, which must fit inside $R=0.032$;
* the **effectiveness of the reuse test**, which decides how many centres reach Stage A — the only
  expensive stage (2020 s of the recorded 4470 s).

Everything downstream ($R$, the §3 frames, the four C grids) is independent of $N$ except through
the number of centres delivered. So the question "why 112?" is exactly: why this resolution rather
than a coarser or finer one?

## 2. The constraints, recomputed

| # | constraint | inequality | consequence | slack at $N=112$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | bin circumradius fits the ball (Prop. 2.10) | $3/(2N)\le R=.032$ | $N\ge46.875$, i.e. $N\ge47$ | $3/224=.01339$, which is $41.9\%$ of $R$ |
| C2 | the X-tuple array fits a signed 32-bit count (§7.3) | $C(N+4,5)<2^{31}$, i.e. $N\le189$ ($C(193,5)=2{,}118{,}000{,}528$; $C(194,5)=2{,}174{,}032{,}288\ge2^{31}$) | $N\le189$ | $C(116,5)=160{,}389{,}488$, i.e. $7.5\%$ of $2^{31}$ |
| C3 | bin differences are stored in `int8_t` (§7.3: "differences lie in $[-111,111]$, including those stored in int8_t") | $N-1\le127$ | $\mathbf{N\le128}$ | $111\le127$ |
| C4 | midpoint gap filter (2.8) against the Lemma 2.5 perturbation $w=1/N$ | thresholds differ by $3/N$, perturbation is $1/N$ | ratio $3$ for **every** $N$ | $3\times$, independent of $N$ |
| C5 | reuse threshold $(\mathrm{fl}(.032N))^2-10^{-4}$ | must dominate the $<10^{-12}$ evaluation error | no bound on $N$ | over-covers by $10^{8}\times$ |
| C6 | $R$'s own ceiling from the §3 frame budget | $2\pi(R+10^{-7})<.203$ | $R\le.032308$, independent of $N$ | $0.96\%$ |

**The window is $[47,128]$, not $[47,189]$.** The 32-bit argument the paper gives does not merely
fail to single out 112 — it is *not the binding constraint*: the paper's own §7.3 `int8_t` claim
caps $N$ at 128, and $C(129,5)=180{,}441{,}264$ is comfortably below $2^{31}$. So the stated reason
for 112 is dominated by an unstated consequence of a storage choice three pages later. That is the
cleanest single sentence to add: *"$N\le128$ because bin differences are stored in `int8_t`;
$N\ge47$ because the bin circumradius must fit the seed ball; the 32-bit bound, $N\le189$, is
weaker than both."*

Two further remarks on the window:
* It is not a continuum: C1 and C3 are the only binding constraints, and they leave 82 admissible
  integers. Nothing in the paper selects 112.
* C4, the filter the paper spends the most space on, is $N$-free. It constrains $R$ relative to
  $w$, not $N$.

## 3. The cost curve

### 3.1 The enumeration side
$C(N+4,5)=N^5/120+O(N^4)$; at $N=112$ it is $160{,}389{,}488$ and the Y side adds
$N\cdot C(N+3,4)=774{,}294{,}080$ tail tests (5.1× the X side, and never reported in Table 1). The
X side alone grows $1.93\times$ from 112 to 128 and $5.8\times$ to 189.

### 3.2 The centre side — the quantity that actually matters
The recorded run is one measured point: **151,744,086 enumerated pairs → 249,878 retained centres**
(Table 1), a reuse factor of 607:1. Why does resolution help? A bin is assimilated into an existing
centre only if the *whole bin* fits in the ball, i.e. if some retained centre is within
$\delta(N):=R-3/(2N)$ of its midpoint. The retained centres are therefore a *maximal
$\delta(N)$-separated subset* of the seed set (a 5-dimensional set in $\mathbb{R}^9$), so

$$M(N)\;\approx\;C_0\,\delta(N)^{-5},\qquad \delta(N)=R-\frac{3}{2N}.$$

Calibrating $C_0$ on the recorded run ($M(112)=249{,}878$) reproduces that point by construction
and predicts everything else. `experiments/n112.py` prints the curve; here it is with the measured
per-unit costs $a=4.096\cdot10^{-6}$ s per pair (621.59 s / 151,744,086) and
$b=8.085\cdot10^{-3}$ s per centre (2020.32 s / 249,878):

The curve is U-shaped with a shallow minimum at $N\approx120$: below it the pair count falls but the
reuse mechanism fails (at $N\le64$ the model retains *every* pair — $\delta$ is so small that no bin
fits inside a ball), above it the pair count dominates. The model is optimistic in the crossover
region (it ignores that centres are lattice points), so treat $N\approx80$ as "reuse has barely
started", not as "96 % reused". **$N=112$ costs 6 % more than the optimum**,
and the optimum is insensitive to the exponent: with $\delta^{-4}$ it is $N=119$ (112 costs 3 %), with
$\delta^{-6}$ it is $N=123$ (112 costs 9 %). `n112.py` prints that sensitivity.

### 3.3 The crossover is the real content
There is a *hard* threshold here, not only a modelled one: two distinct bin midpoints are at least
$1/N$ apart in some coordinate, and a bin is assimilated into an existing centre only if the whole
bin fits, i.e. only if the centre is within $\delta(N)=R-3/(2N)$. So reuse is **impossible** unless
$\delta(N)\ge1/N$, i.e. $R\ge5/(2N)$, i.e. $N\ge78.125$: below $N=79$ the clustering of centres
provably does nothing and every admissible pair becomes a Stage-A seed. The model's crossover
($N\approx80$) agrees with this bound, which is some evidence for the model.

That is the argument the paper is missing, and it is a better one than the 32-bit remark: **the
resolution has to be fine enough for the reuse test to fire at all, $N\ge79$, and 112 sits
two-thirds of the way up the window with the whole useful range above the crossover.** Equivalently, the "effective separation" $\delta=R-3/(2N)$ has to be a decent
fraction of $R$: .419 at $N=112$, .366 at 128, .248 at 189, .087 at 80.

## 4. What breaks at half and at double

**$N=56$.** Coverage survives: $3/112=.0268<.032$, slack $1.19\times$. The 32-bit and int8 bounds
are comfortable. What breaks is the *cost*: $\delta=0.005214$, so the model predicts no reuse at all
— all $5{,}461{,}512$ pairs become centres, which at 8.1 ms per centre is $\approx12.3$ hours of
Stage A on four cores instead of 34 minutes. Each centre also needs its own atlas and its own
records, so the $10^{6}$-node cap, the $2\cdot10^{6}$-Root cap and the depth-6 cap would all be
reached, and reaching a cap in Stage A produces *unresolved* seeds — i.e. no theorem. Halving $N$ is
therefore a soundness-preserving but conclusion-destroying change; it is not "cheaper".

**$N=224$.** Coverage is fine and reuse is excellent ($\delta=.0253$). Two things break:
(i) bin differences reach 223, so the `int8_t` store claimed in §7.3 overflows — the cover as
specified cannot be built; (ii) $C(228,5)=4{,}912{,}679{,}520>2^{31}$, so the 32-bit argument also
fails. Cost would be $7.7\times$ the current run. Doubling $N$ is not available without changing the
storage type and the counter widths, i.e. without changing the paper's arithmetic premises.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Keep $N=112$.** It is within 6 % of the modelled cost optimum and two-thirds of the way up the
   admissible window, so it is defensible as a cost choice. Replace the 32-bit sentence by the
   three-line derivation above ($N\ge47$ from the ball, $N\le128$ from `int8_t`, 112 chosen for cost).
2. **If a single change is wanted, use 128**: the largest admissible value, and dyadic, so every
   midpoint phase $(b+\tfrac12)/128$ is exact in binary64 and the $0.0320001$ enlargement becomes
   unnecessary. The trade is $1.93\times$ the enumeration for $0.65\times$ the centres and about the
   same wall time.
3. **Correct `results/plan.md` §3 (T5) and §9(3)**, which state the window as $[47,189]$: the
   binding upper bound is 128, not 189. `results/code-audit.md` §4 lists "integer overflow in the
   grid products" as a red-team item but does not draw this consequence.
4. **Report the measured centre curve** if the authors can produce it: the single number 249,878 is
   the only calibration point, and one run at $N=128$ would settle the model's exponent.

## 6. Limits of this analysis

The centre curve is a *model*, calibrated at one point, with the 5-dimensional packing law as its
only structural assumption. It is not a measurement of the paper's pipeline (the authors' code is
not in the packet), and the exponent is carried as a parameter with an explicit sensitivity band
rather than measured: at the paper's $\delta=0.0186$ a direct sampling of a 5-manifold would need
$\delta^{-5}\approx4\cdot10^{8}$ points. What the model does establish is that the cost is
U-shaped, that its minimum is near 120 for any exponent in $\{4,5,6\}$, and that below
$N\approx70$ the reuse mechanism provably cannot fire at all (that part is not a model: it follows
from $\delta(N)<1/N$ for $N<70$, since then no bin can be closer to a centre than the bin spacing).

## 7. One inconsistency in the cover's own counts

`seedcover.py` recomputes the enumeration. §2.3 says `seeds::build` "visits every weakly increasing
five-bin X tuple and applies (2.7), the first inequality in (2.8), and sumok". The orientation filter
(2.7), $a\le l-d+1$, is integral and applies to every tuple, so the number of surviving X pairs is
at most

$$|\{(a,b,c,d,l):0\le a\le b\le c\le d\le l<112,\ a\le l-d+1\}| = 85{,}349{,}292$$

(53.2 % of $C(116,5)$; adding the (2.8) midpoint-gap filter leaves 16,197,157). Table 1 reports
**151,744,086 pairs**, i.e. 94.6 % of $C(116,5)$ — more than the filters can leave by a factor
1.78, and less than the pre-filter total by 8,645,402. So either "pair" means something the paper
never defines, or the count is wrong. This matters because it is the *only* place where the cover's
completeness is backed by a number that could be checked against the enumeration; Prop. 2.10 itself
is argued structurally ("the tuple is present in the stated enumeration") and would survive, but the
one auditable figure does not agree with the stated filters. The fix is one sentence plus two
numbers: report the pre-filter total, the post-filter total, and the number of matched (X,Y) pairs.
