# Reading note: *The maximum number of mutually unbiased bases in dimension six* (OpenAI, 24 Sep 2026, 76 pp.)

\[
N(6)=\max\{\text{size of a family of pairwise mutually unbiased orthonormal bases of } \mathbb{C}^6\}.
\]

## 1. What is claimed

* **Theorem 1.1 (computer-assisted).** There are no four pairwise mutually unbiased orthonormal bases in $\mathbb{C}^6$; with the explicit tensor construction of **Lemma 1.2** ($N(6)\ge \min(2,3)+1=3$) this gives $N(6)=3$, i.e. the dimension-six case of Zauner's conjecture.
* **Corollary 1.3.** At most three pairwise Killing-orthogonal, adjoint-closed Cartan subalgebras of $\mathfrak{sl}_6(\mathbb{C})$, citing Boykin–Sitharam–Tiep–Wocjan for the correspondence. (Two bases are unbiased iff the Cartan subalgebras $\mathrm{span}\{bb^*-I/6\}$ are Killing-orthogonal, since $\operatorname{tr}((bb^*-I/6)(cc^*-I/6))=|\langle b,c\rangle|^2-1/6$.)
* **Status.** The theorem is stated conditionally on (i) binary64 / compiler / libm premises (App. A, §7.1), (ii) a *specified, complete* execution of seven unchanged C++ sources (100 invocations, ~75 min) whose records are archived and hash-identified, (iii) that the sources implement the specification the lemmas describe. The mathematical content of the paper is the collection of *necessity* lemmas plus their numerical remainders; the *sufficiency* (that the search actually closes) is not proved, only observed.

## 2. The spine of the argument

**Global shape.** One unitary normalizes the first basis; the second becomes $H/\sqrt6$ with $H$ a complex Hadamard matrix (all entries modulus 1, $HH^*=6I$). The problem is to exclude an extension of $(\text{coordinate basis},\,H/\sqrt6)$ by two further bases *for every* $H$. The proof is a five-layer cover: cover the second basis by finitely many balls of real phases, then exclude, uniformly on each ball, the existence of two extra bases.

**(0) Reductions and symmetries (§2.1–2.2).** Extendibility is invariant under row/column permutations and rephasings, transpose, and conjugation (**2.2**). Dephasing ($D_0$), and double centering $\Pi$ onto the "horizontal" space (zero row and column sums) give equivalent representatives (**2.3**: $\Pi$ is the orthogonal projection, norm non-increasing). **Lemma 2.4** is the combinatorial heart of the seed stage: take a *minimum circular gap* among all row pairs and column pairs of $H$, make it the first row pair (transposing if it was a column pair), dephase, sort along the complementary arc; then $x=(0,x_1,\dots,x_5)$ with $0\le x_1\le\cdots\le x_5$, gap $g_x=1-x_5$, and an orientation inequality $x_1\le x_5-x_4$ (achieved by swapping the pair and re-reading the circle); after choosing one of the five non-initial columns and moving it to second position, the second column is $y=(0,\xi,y_2,\dots,y_5)$ with the shared entry $\xi=x_s$. Orthogonality gives two vanishing sums $1+\sum_j e(x_j)=0$, $1+e(\xi)+\sum_j e(y_j)=0$, and $g_y\ge g_x$.

**(1) Finite seed cover (§2.2–2.4).** Bin $[0,1]$ at $N=112$, closed. Necessary filters: midpoint gap inequalities (**2.6**), a Taylor-based vanishing-sum test with all-direction support bounds (**2.7**), and shared-coordinate interval constraints intersected between the $x$- and $y$-lists (**2.8**). Centers are not all kept: a dual/support bound (**2.9**, evaluated at heuristically proposed $v$ but valid for the stored $v$) certifies that a whole bin pair's feasible part lies within radius $0.032$ of an earlier center. **Proposition 2.10**: the retained rational midpoints, radius $0.032$ ($0.0320001$ after conversion to binary phases), cover the nine seed phases of *every* exact $H$ up to the equivalences of 2.2. Run: 151,744,086 bin pairs $\to$ 249,878 centers.

**(2) Stage A: all completions of a seed (§3).** Split $H$ into $2+4$ blocks. At a seed center the blocks $B_0$ (upper right) and $C_0^*$ (lower left) have rank 2, giving fixed kernels $\ker B_0$, $\ker C_0^*$ (**3.1**, used unchanged across the whole seed ball; no exact solution is assumed at the center). Compress the unknown $4\times4$ block: $Y=K_C^*D'K_B$ satisfies $6I-Y^*Y\succeq0$ with trace defect $\le q=R_s^2(1+(A_*^2+6)/m_C^2)<0.32$ (**3.2**), so the polar factor $V\in U(2)$ exists and $\|Y-\sqrt6 V\|_F\le p$. Hence **every** completion of the seed gives a point of $U(2)$: cover $U(2)$ by the closed Euler-type cells of **3.3** ($\alpha\in[0,\tfrac12]$, $t\in[0,\tfrac14]$, $\beta,\gamma\in[0,1]$; $N_u/2\cdot N_u/4\cdot N_u\cdot N_u$ cells) with uniform Taylor bounds (**3.4**; note the $\cos^2+\sin^2$ bookkeeping giving exactly $42h^4$). Then a *comparison block* $D=D_{\text{span}}+D_{\text{ker}}$ is fixed per cell, $Z=D'-D$ splits into three Frobenius-orthogonal pieces (**3.5**, $\|Z\|_F\le z$), a *linear comparison* $J(s,\theta)$ from the first variations with a uniform remainder (**3.6–3.7**, $\|Z-J\|_F\le E_r$). Two necessary tests follow: a norm/flatness test and a **dual (separating-hyperplane) test** with 20 stacked equations (**3.8–3.9**); a violating direction $y$ is a certificate regardless of how $y$ was found (**3.10**). **Proposition 3.11**: if the finite cell recursion terminates with no unresolved cell, every feasible completion lies in a certified phase ball. Grid $N_u=16\to32\to64\to\dots\to512$, node caps, one million extra-node cap.

**(3) Certified phase balls and the atlas (§4).** A **Root certificate** (Def. 4.1) is a center $P\in\mathbb R^{36}$, a numerical tangent frame $T$ ($k\le10$), and constants $(C,n_0,id,ort,hor,mnorm,froot)$ such that, for *every* horizontal $\delta$ with $F(P+\delta)=0$ ($F$ = the 15 complex row-orthogonality residuals), one has $\|(I-TT^*)\delta\|\le n_0+id\,s+C s^2$ with $s=\|\delta\|$, a squared inequality recovering $\|\delta\|$ from $\|T^*\delta\|$, and a *difference* bound comparing two centers. Construction (§4.2) uses a stored approximate inverse $M$ of $L_P\Pi$ and a checked Gram defect; quadratic remainders are bounded by a tensor-norm trick with two computable constants (**4.3–4.5**). Transport under signed permutations/transpose (**4.7**), exact center alignment modulo row/column rephasings (**4.17**), arc/weighted-arc bounds turning $\|Z\|_F\le z$ into phase displacement (**4.8**), an explicit center-correction budget $\|\epsilon\|\le2\cdot10^{-6}$ (**4.9**), then **direct and tangent (ellipsoidal) displacement bounds** (**4.10–4.12**) and a finite *bootstrap* that only ever decreases the radius (**4.13**). Balls are merged into a global atlas by a triangle inequality (**4.15**). **Proposition 4.16**: with the seed cover valid and no failed seed, the atlas covers every exact second-basis Hadamard matrix up to extendibility-preserving equivalence. Radii here are ~0.125.

**(4) Stage B: shrinking the balls (§5).** Uses the difference bound between two Root certificates to prove a *certified relation*: given a bound $t$ on $\|T^*\delta\|$ in the parent, an upper bound on $\|\delta-d_q\|$ at the child (**5.2**), then a monotone upper-bound iteration needing neither contraction nor a fixed point (**5.3**), then an exhaustive **grid in tangent coordinates** ($k\le10$, base boxes, spanning-tree-free subdivision, farthest-corner bounds) with outside tests, and a *blocker induction*: parents whose refinement completed are queried at their fixed radius (**5.7**). Failed parents stay on the right-hand side as obligations. Batching writes atlas records at the target radius $\tau$ and is covered by **Cor. 5.8**. Three passes $(\tau,w)=(0.07,0.013),(0.016,0.005),(0.011,0.0038)$.

**(5) Stage C: exclude two further bases inside a ball (§6).** Vectors unbiased to the coordinate basis are $e(\beta)/\sqrt6$ up to global phase, so a closed-bin search in five phases enumerates them; bins are tested with single-vector necessary inequalities (**6.1–6.2**, sharpened weighted Taylor form), kept bins are refined $12\to24\to48\to96$ and clustered into **closed vector balls** $B_{\text{vec}}(v,s)$ (**6.4**), which are pairwise "neither orthogonal nor unbiased" for $s<0.1$ (**6.5**) — so twelve vectors in two further bases need **twelve distinct balls**. Two graphs on ball labels: $O$ (orthogonality not excluded), $U$ (unbiasedness not excluded) (**6.4**, direct and Taylor support tests; **6.5** joint test with the *shared* Hadamard displacement $\eta$). Two further bases would force two disjoint $O$-six-cliques with all 36 cross edges in $U$ (**6.6**, (6.24)). A finite clique search with two pools and exact bit sets decides this pattern (**6.8**), and a negative result is a genuine exclusion (**6.9**). Work caps, bin overflow, clustering failure = *unresolved*, never negative.

**(6) Execution and the certificate (§7, App. A).** Theorem 7.1 turns "all terminal conditions of the specified invocation script hold, with record provenance and batch checks" into "no four MUBs". The seven sources, four entry points, byte-level record format, build commands, platform probes, one codegen check (the two `pow(·,2)` calls must be lowered to multiplications; verified in a saved disassembly at named addresses), sharded invocation list, terminal-condition list, and a table of counts and SHA-256s. Appendix A is ~20 pages of one-sided binary64 error analysis: per-expression absolute-error budgets, "10^-13 error vs 10^-8 margin" style, with a final table collecting every established error next to its explicit margin.

## 3. The technical idea worth carrying away

The proof is built on a strict separation between **proposing** and **certifying**. A floating-point center need not be an exact Hadamard matrix; a computed eigenspace need not parametrize anything; a Cholesky or least-squares solve need not be accurate. Every artefact is used only after an *independent* inequality check that is one-sided (outward) with an explicit slack, and every exclusion is a strict violation of a *necessary* condition for an exact solution. Consequences: overlaps, duplicate representatives, redundant balls and heuristic search orders are harmless; anything not certified becomes *unresolved*, and unresolved is never a negative. This is why the argument does not need rank constancy, manifold structure, or a canonical representative — and it is what allows the outer cover to survive the refinement stages.

## 5. Areas for improvement

Ordered by how much they would change what the paper establishes.

### (a) The trust base is process, not proof — make the certificate checkable by a small verified checker
The theorem's hypothesis is an *event* (a 100-process run whose log and hashes are in an archive) plus the assumption that seven C++ files implement the spec of Sections 2–6. A referee cannot check either. The architecture is already 90% ready for the standard fix: every exclusion is a violation of a *necessary* condition, so the pipeline can be demoted to a heuristic **generator** and the conclusion made to rest on a **small checker** that consumes the record stream (the format is specified in §7.1) and re-verifies each local inequality: Root constants, per-cell exclusion certificates, per-bin and per-edge tests, coverage radii. Concretely:

* ship the *witnesses*, not just the verdicts — e.g. the $O/U$ adjacency sets (≤2048 vertices, 32 words each) for the 22,379 Stage-C verdicts, so the negative clique searches can be re-run independently; currently "no 32-word pattern" is asserted only by the program's own search;
* make the seed cover independently checkable (it almost is: each bin pair is either killed by a *local* filter or covered by a *local* comparison with a retained center — a second implementation over `centers.txt` should reproduce the 249,878 centers);
* then formalize the checker (Lean/Coq over exact rational arithmetic). What remains unformalized is only *search completeness* (that no region was silently dropped: enumeration of bins, subdivision, atlas immutability, unresolved counting) — which is exactly the part §7.2–7.3 already specifies in detail and which is small enough to be audited or formalized too.

As it stands, one sign error anywhere in ~4,000 lines — say in the negative part $\max(0,\max_{i,j}(-y_{D,ij}))$ of (3.29), or in the two-pool root deletion of Lemma 6.8 — produces a *silent* false exclusion, and the paper gives the reader no way to detect it.

### (b) Replace binary64-plus-margins by directed-rounding interval arithmetic
Appendix A is ~20 pages of per-expression absolute-error budgets tied to a specific evaluation order, plus a build that is `-march=native` (so not portable, and a *different* binary), plus a spot check that two `pow(x,2)` calls were lowered to multiplications by inspecting a saved disassembly at named hex addresses. The paper itself concedes the platform probes are "limited evidence ... not a proof of all primitive-operation or compiler semantics". This is a large amount of fragile argument for a small gain. If all certificate evaluations used outward-rounded interval arithmetic (MPFR with directed rounding, or rational endpoints, or a small verified interval library), then "the stored number is an upper bound" would be a theorem of the library rather than of 20 pages of case analysis, the compiler dependence and the disassembly check would disappear, and the whole arithmetic appendix would collapse to a few pages. The cost is runtime, and the run is only ~75 minutes.

### (c) There are no controls and no robustness statistics
For a computation whose entire force is "no region survived", the paper reports only terminal outcomes (0 unresolved). Missing:

* **lower-dimensional controls** — an analogue of the pipeline in $d=4$ (also a $2+2$ block split) *must fail to exclude* a fourth basis, since five exist; in $d=3,4$ it should also fail. A pipeline that "proves" too much there is broken. This is the single cheapest bug-catcher for the exclusion logic;
* **known-case reproduction** — Grassl's theorem (standard + Fourier pair is not extendible) and Jaming–Matolcsi–Móra–Szöllősi–Weiner for the Fourier family are rigorous antecedents; Stage C run on those specific $H$ should exclude them for the *expected* reason, not by cap exhaustion;
* **slack distributions** — the final table gives "established error vs explicit margin", but not how close accepted certificates came to their thresholds. If some Root ball sits at $z=1.499$ against $1.5$, or some bin at $|S|^2$ within $10^{-9}$ of a threshold, the result is ulp-fragile; report min slack (and the argmin) per test type;
* **distance to caps** — max subdivision depth reached, max nodes per seed, max graph steps used (163 records hit the 5,000,000-step cap before fallback). These tell a reader how close the run is to the boundary of its own admissibility;
* **mutation tests** — deliberately perturb a bound by $+10^{-9}$ and confirm the pipeline then reports unresolved rather than success, i.e. that the checks are tight enough to notice;
* **independent recomputation of a sample** — 10–100 records re-verified at 200-digit precision in a separate implementation.

### (d) State a finite theorem, and clean up the provenance
Theorem 1.1 quantifies over "the arithmetic and compiler conditions in Appendix A" and "the fresh complete execution documented in Section 7.5" — a hypothesis by reference to an event. The mathematical statement should be finite and inspectable: *given a certificate file $C$ with SHA-256 $h$ and a checker that accepts $C$, no four MUBs exist in $\mathbb{C}^6$*; and separately *$C$ exists and is archived at ...*. Related hygiene problems:

* the manuscript is dated **24 Sep 2026** but the run it rests on is dated **4 Oct 2026**, and §1.2/§7.5 disown a **7 Sep 2026** run ("the complete evidence packet ... was not recovered", "this agreement does not authenticate the earlier execution"). A reader cannot tell what the 24 Sep version asserted, or why an unauthenticated earlier run is discussed at all. Either drop the earlier run or explain precisely what changed;
* the abstract's "resolving Zauner's dimension-six MUB conjecture" is the standard attribution (the conjecture $N(6)\le3$ is indeed Zauner's) but should be stated as "the upper bound", with the lower bound credited to the tensor construction, and the relation to the 2026 order-six classification [7] (which is about individual Hadamard matrices, not MUB compatibility) made explicit rather than dismissive;
* the invocation parameters ($\tau,w$, $M=12$, $N_u$, caps) look tuned to this run; a table of one-parameter perturbations (which caps and which parameters the result survives) would show the conclusion is not knife-edge at the algorithmic level.

### (e) Presentation: three layers interleaved, and code behaviour asserted in prose
Each of §§3–6 mixes (i) ideal mathematics, (ii) statements about what a particular C++ routine does (`extend` "begins with $[-1/2,1/2]$ and intersects these constraints", `spectralUpper` "uses their minimum with margins", `markRec` "keeps a branch only while ..."), and (iii) numerical budgets. (ii) is unverifiable from the document — no code appears, and a lemma's validity often depends on it. Suggested restructuring: a mathematics paper (statements whose hypotheses are code-free), a **specification** section giving each routine's contract in pretension form (pre/post as inequalities), and a verification report; plus (1) a table mapping every lemma to routine(s) and to the Appendix subsection that certifies its margins, (2) a dependency graph of the five stages showing exactly which conditionality is discharged where, (3) a code appendix with the checker-sized fragments. Notation needs a table of its own: "Root / Record / cell / box / bin / sphere", $D$ (comparison block, §3–4.4) vs $\hat D(t)$ (Stage B iteration), $n_P$ (normal polynomial, then normal displacement), $\epsilon$ (center correction in cycles) vs $\varepsilon_{exp}$, $m$ (pair of lower singular bounds) vs $m_j=|D_j|$. Also: 76 pages with ~50 lemmas and pervasive "as proved in Section A" makes the argument hard to audit even where it is correct; the sections' *conclusions* (2.10, 3.11, 4.16, 5.7, 6.9, 7.1) deserve to be collected in one place as the spine.

### (f) Smaller points
* **Corollary 1.3** (Lie algebra) adds nothing to the MUB claim and rests on a 2005 preprint; either prove the correspondence or drop it.
* Many constants are deliberately crude ($z<1.5$ where the truth is far smaller; $\pi b$ remainders), which inflates subdivision depth, the record count, and the number of Stage-C graph-cap failures. Sharper bounds or interval arithmetic in the generator would cut the pipeline substantially and make it easier to re-run.
* The cell parametrization of $U(2)$ is redundant (${\sim}8\times$ over-counting) and each completion may lie in many cells; harmless for soundness, but a reduced parametrization (or a canonical cell choice) would shrink Stage A.
* "Every interval cell may be taken closed" is used throughout and is correct, but the paper never states the resulting global convention (closed sets, $\cup$-covers, no measure-theoretic shortcuts) in one place.
