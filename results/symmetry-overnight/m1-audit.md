written, and both gaps are repairable:

1. the **rank-one recovery lemma** — the source's proof stops in the "uniform" case with

## 1. Conventions

`A, B, C` are MUBs of `C^6`. In `A`-coordinates write the coefficient matrices of `B`, `C` as
the unitaries `U = H_B/sqrt6`, `V = H_C/sqrt6`, so `|U_{ij}| = |V_{ij}| = 1/sqrt6`. Put

* `W_B = { sum_r x_r b_r b_r^* : sum_r x_r = 0 }` (traceless operators diagonal in `B`), dim 5;
* `P_A` = orthogonal projection (matrix inner product `(X,Y) = tr X^*Y`) onto `W_B ⊕ W_C` inside
  the 30-dimensional space of off-diagonal matrix units `E_{ij}` (`i ≠ j`).

`Lambda_0 = {x in Z^6 : sum_i x_i = 0} ≅ Z^5`. `L_A ⊆ Lambda_0` is the subgroup generated by all
`r_{ij,kl} = e_i - e_j - e_k + e_l` with `(P_A)_{ij,kl} ≠ 0`. `R_A` is the matrix of those rows
after deleting the sixth coordinate, reduced mod 3. `G_A` = diagonal unitaries mod scalars that
fix `A`'s rays and preserve the ray sets of `B` and `C`; `D_A` = diagonal unitaries mod scalars
preserving `P_A` (i.e. `T P_A T^* = P_A`). `Delta_A = |G_A|`.

## 2. Theorem A: formula (1) is the projection  [proved] [exact]

**Claim.** For `i ≠ j`, `k ≠ l`,
`(P_A)_{ij,kl} = sum_r U_{ir} conj(U_{jr}) conj(U_{kr}) U_{lr} + sum_r V_{ir} conj(V_{jr}) conj(V_{kr}) V_{lr}`.
Moreover `P_A^2 = P_A = P_A^*`, `(P_A)_{ij,ij} = 1/3`, `rank P_A = 10`.

**Proof.** `sum_r b_r b_r^* = I`, so subtracting the trace part gives the frame
`w_r := b_r b_r^* - I/6`, whose entries are `(w_r)_{ij} = U_{ir} conj(U_{jr})` for `i ≠ j` and
`0` on the diagonal. The Gram matrix is
`(w_r,w_s) = |(b_r,b_s)|^2 - 1/6 = delta_{rs} - 1/6`, i.e. `G = I - J/6`, which is *itself* the
orthogonal projection onto `{sum x_r = 0}`; hence `G^{-1}` on its range is `G` and
`P_B := sum_{rs} G_{rs} w_r w_s^* = sum_r w_r w_r^* - (1/6) sum_{rs} w_r w_s^* = sum_r w_r w_r^*`,
the last term vanishing because `sum_s w_s = 0`. Taking entries with `i ≠ j`, `k ≠ l` gives (1)
(the subtraction term `(1/6)(sum_r U_{ir}conj(U_{jr}))(sum_s conj(U_{ks})U_{ls})` vanishes since
each factor is `delta_{ij} = 0` resp. `delta_{kl} = 0`). The same computation for `V`, and
`(w^B_r, w^C_s) = |(b_r,c_s)|^2 - 1/6 = 0`, so `P_A = P_B + P_C` is the projection onto
`W_B ⊕ W_C`; it is Hermitian and idempotent. Its trace is
`sum_{i≠j} (P_A)_{ij,ij} = 30 · 2·6·(1/36) = 10 = rank` (idempotent ⇒ diagonalisable ⇒ trace =
rank). QED

**Machine check [exact].** In `Z[zeta_24]` (`m1-scripts/final_checks.py`), for the standard product
triple at all three anchors: `36P` is Hermitian, `(36P)^2 = 36·(36P)` entrywise, `tr(36P) = 360`
(⇔ `tr P = 10`), `36P_{ss} = 12` (⇔ `1/3`), and — the check that (1) is the *right* projection
and not merely some idempotent — `P_A w = w` for all twelve flat traceless matrices
`w = b_r b_r^* - I/6`, `c_s c_s^* - I/6`.

## 3. Theorem B: the recovery lemma, repaired  [proved]

**Claim.** For two MUBs `B, C` in `C^d`, `d > 2`, the only rank-one projectors in
`R I + W_B + W_C` are the `2d` basis projectors `b_r b_r^*`, `c_s c_s^*`.

**Proof.** Let `rho` be such a rank-one projector; write `rho = lambda I + w_B + w_C` with
`w_B in W_B`, `w_C in W_C`, `lambda = 1/d`. The `B`-diagonal of `w_C` vanishes (unbiasedness:
`(b_i, w_C b_i) = sum_r x_r |c_{ri}|^2 = (1/d) sum_r x_r = 0`), hence the `B`-diagonal of `rho` is
`D_p = lambda I + w_B` with `p_i = (b_i, rho b_i) in [0,1]`, `sum p_i = 1`, and `rho - D_p = w_C in W_C`.
Taking the `B`-diagonal of `rho^2 = rho`: `p_i^2 + (Y^2)_{ii} = p_i` where `Y = w_C` is Hermitian and
diagonal in the `C` basis, so `(Y^2)_{ii} = sum_j |Y_{ij}|^2 = (1/d) tr Y^2` for every `i` (again
`|c_{rj}|^2 = 1/d`). Hence `p_i^2 - p_i + tau = 0` with `tau = tr(Y^2)/d = sum_r x_r^2 / d >= 0`. All
`p_i` solve one quadratic, so `p_i in {r, 1-r}`, `r(1-r) = tau`; if a `p_i` is the larger root and
`r > 0` then `1 = sum p_i >= (1-r) + (d-1)r = 1 + (d-2)r > 1` because `d > 2`. So either all
`p_i = r`, or `p_i in {0,1}` and the `p`'s are a delta.

*Delta case:* `p_i = 1` for one `i` (since `sum p_i = 1`) gives `(b_i, rho b_i) = 1`, which for a
rank-one projector forces `rho = b_i b_i^*`.
*Uniform case* (this is the step the source leaves out): all `p_i = 1/d`. Then the `B`-diagonal
part of `rho` is `(1/d)I`, i.e. `w_B = 0`, and `rho = (1/d)I + w_C` is **diagonal in the `C` basis**
with entries `1/d + x_r`; as `rho` is a projection these lie in `{0,1}`, and `sum_r (1/d + x_r) = 1`
forces exactly one entry `1`, so `rho = c_r c_r^*`. QED

(Sharpness: `d = 2` fails — `B = F_2`, `C = diag(1,i)F_2` — and the inequality `1+(d-2)r > 1` is
exactly where `d > 2` enters.)

**Use.** With `T` diagonal and `T P_A T^* = P_A`, `T` preserves `R I` (trivially) and
`W_B ⊕ W_C`, hence preserves `R I + W_B + W_C` and therefore permutes the twelve rank-one
projectors; the induced map `D_A → Sym(12)` is injective, because `T b_r = mu_r b_r` for a
diagonal `T` with `b_{r,i} ≠ 0` for all `i` forces `T = mu_r I`. Hence `D_A` is **finite**.

## 4. Theorem C: the lattice theorem  [proved]

**(i)** `D_A ≅ Hom(Lambda_0/L_A, T)`. *Proof.* `T = diag(lambda)` (mod scalars) acts on matrix
units by `T E_{ij} T^* = lambda_i conj(lambda_j) E_{ij}`, so `T P_A T^* = P_A` iff
`lambda_i conj(lambda_j) conj(lambda_k) lambda_l = 1` for every nonzero `(P_A)_{ij,kl}` iff
`chi_r(lambda) = 1` for all `r in L_A`, where `chi_r(lambda) = prod_i lambda_i^{r_i}`.
Parametrise projective diagonal unitaries by `a_i = lambda_i/lambda_6` (`i <= 5`), a bijection
onto `T^5`; since `sum_i r_i = 0`, `chi_r(lambda) = prod_{i<=5} a_i^{r_i}`, so the condition is
exactly that `a` lies in the annihilator of `L_A ≅ Z^5`, i.e. `D_A = Hom(Z^5/L_A, T)`. QED

**(ii)** `rank L_A = 5` and `|D_A| = [Lambda_0 : L_A]`. *Proof.* By Theorem B and the injectivity
in §3, `D_A` embeds in `Sym(12)`, hence is finite. If `rank L_A < 5` then `Hom(Z^5/L_A, T)`
contains a torus `T^{5-rank L_A}` and is infinite. So `rank L_A = 5`, `Z^5/L_A` is finite of
order `[Lambda_0:L_A]`, and `|Hom(finite, T)| = |Z^5/L_A|`. QED

**(iii)** `3 | [Lambda_0:L_A] ⟺ rank_{F_3} R_A < 5`. *Proof.* Tensor
`0 → L_A → Lambda_0 → Lambda_0/L_A → 0` with `F_3`:
`F_3^5 / im(L_A ⊗ F_3) ≅ (Lambda_0/L_A) ⊗ F_3 ≠ 0 ⟺ 3 | |Lambda_0/L_A|`, and `im` is spanned by
the relation vectors mod 3. QED (No Smith-form computation is needed; in the examples of §8 the
Smith diagonal of `R_A` is `(1,1,1,1,index)`.)

**(iv)** `Delta_A in {1,2,3,6}` and `G_A` is cyclic. *Proof.* Every non-identity element of `G_A`
acts freely on the six `B`-rays (fixing one forces `T` scalar), so all orbits have size `|G_A|`,
hence `|G_A|` divides 6; `G_A` is a subgroup of a torus, hence abelian, hence cyclic of order
dividing 6. QED

**(v)** `D_A = G_A` in `d = 6` — this is Theorem D; consequently `Delta_A = |D_A| = [Lambda_0:L_A]`
exactly, and `3 | Delta_A ⟺` a nonconstant order-three clock exists at `A` (Theorem E).

Without Theorem D one only has `|D_A| in {Delta_A, 2 Delta_A}` (a swap, if it existed, would be a
coset), and the source's (5) could be off by a factor 2 exactly at anchors with `Delta_A in {1,2}`
— precisely the anchors that matter. (The congruence `3 | Delta_A ⟺ 3 | |D_A|` is unaffected.)

## 5. Theorem D: no diagonal exchange in dimension six  [proved]

**Claim.** Let `A,B,C` be MUBs of `C^6` and `T` a diagonal unitary (mod scalars) with
`T(B) = C` and `T(C) = B` as ray sets. No such `T` exists.

**Lemma D1.** Any such diagonal swap satisfies `|tr T|^2 = 6`.
*Proof.* `T b_r = mu_r c_{pi(r)}` for a permutation `pi` and phases `mu_r`. In `A`-coordinates
`lambda_i b_{r,i} = mu_r c_{pi(r),i}`, and `|b_{r,i}| = 1/sqrt6`, so
`lambda_i = 6 mu_r c_{pi(r),i} conj(b_{r,i})`. Summing over `i` for fixed `r`:
`tr T = 6 mu_r (b_r, c_{pi(r)})`, and `|(b_r,c_{pi(r)})| = 1/sqrt6`, so `|tr T| = sqrt6`. QED

**Lemma D2 (order reduction).** A swap has projective order dividing 12, and some odd power of it
is a swap of order 2, 3 or 4, still with `|tr|^2 = 6`.
*Proof.* `T^2(B) = T(C) = B` and `T^2(C) = B`, so `T^2` preserves each of `B, C` and fixes `A`'s
rays, i.e. `T^2 in G_A`, whose order divides 6; so `T^12` is a scalar. `T^k(B) = C` iff `k` is
odd, and for `m = ord(T) in {2,3,4,6,12}` an odd `k` gives order `m/gcd(m,k) in {2,3,4}`
(`m = 6: k = 3`; `m = 12: k = 3`). D1 applies to every odd power. QED

**Lemma D3.** No diagonal unitary `T` with `|tr T|^2 = 6` has projective order 2, 3 or 4.
*Proof.* Order 2: `tr T = m_+ - m_- in Z`, so `|tr T|^2 in {0,4,16,36} ≠ 6`. Order 4:
`tr T = a + bi` with `a,b in Z`, and `a^2 + b^2 = 6` is impossible (squares mod 8 are 0,1,4).
Order 3: eigenvalue multiplicities `(n_0,n_1,n_2)`, `sum n_i = 6`, give
`|tr T|^2 = sum n_i^2 - sum_{i<j} n_i n_j = 36 - 3p` with `p = sum_{i<j} n_i n_j`; `|tr T|^2 = 6`
needs `p = 10`, but the partitions of 6 give `p in {0,5,8,9,9,11,12}`
(`(6,0,0),(5,1,0),(4,2,0),(4,1,1),(3,3,0),(3,2,1),(2,2,2)`). QED

Theorem D follows from D1–D3. QED

**Remarks.**
* Order 12 is *not* excluded by the multiplicity count alone: exactly 120 multiplicity vectors
  `n in Z_{>=0}^{12}`, `sum n_j = 6`, satisfy `|sum n_j zeta_12^j|^2 = 6`
  (`m1-scripts/noswap.py`). That is why the odd-power reduction D2 is not cosmetic; the source's
  phrase "reducing its projective order to two or four" is doing exactly this, but the reduction
  must be stated and the order-3 case handled separately.
* `d = 2` is a genuine exception: `A` standard, `B = F_2`, `C = diag(1,i)B`, `T = diag(1,i)`
  swaps `B` and `C`. In `d = 3` the order-3 count has the solution `(2,1,0)` (`p = 2`), so the
  lemma is genuinely a `d = 6` statement, as the source says.
* The statement holds with the anchors exchanged, so it applies at all three anchors; this is
  what direction 2's use of "an order-three unitary cannot exchange the two bases" needs.

## 6. Theorem E: criterion (3) and the Xi equivalence  [proved]

**(a) Criterion (3).** A clock `T = diag(w^{a_1},...,w^{a_6})`, `a in F_3^6`, satisfies
`T P_A T^* = P_A` iff `a_i - a_j - a_k + a_l ≡ 0 (mod 3)` for all nonzero `(P_A)_{ij,kl}`.
Adding a constant to all `a_i` multiplies `T` by a scalar, so we may set `a_6 = 0`; then, with
`a'_i = a_i - a_6` (`i <= 5`), the system is `R_A a' = 0` over `F_3`, and a *nonconstant* clock
corresponds bijectively to `0 ≠ a' in ker R_A`. Hence `∃` nonconstant order-three clock at `A`
`⟺ rank_{F_3} R_A < 5`. QED

By Theorem D such a clock preserves each basis, so it lies in `G_A` and has order 3; conversely
an order-3 element of `G_A` is such a clock. Combined with Theorem C:
`3 | Delta_A ⟺ rank_{F_3} R_A < 5 ⟺ ∃` nonconstant order-three diagonal symmetry at `A`.

**(b) Xi.** `Xi(P) = sum_{|I|=5, det(r_s : s in I) ≢ 0 (3)} prod_{s in I} |P_s|^2` is a finite sum
of nonnegative terms, so `Xi(P) > 0` iff five nonzero entries of `P` carry five relations
independent mod 3, i.e. iff `rank_{F_3} R_A = 5`; hence `Xi(P_A) = 0 ⟺ rank_{F_3} R_A < 5 ⟺` a
threefold symmetry at `A` (source (12)). This is immediate from (a) and is not the difficulty;
(13) is.

**(c) The eighty types [exact].** Enumerating all `(i,j,k,l)`, `i≠j`, `k≠l`, projectivising
`r mod 3` by `r ~ -r`, gives exactly **80** classes: 15 of shape `2(e_i-e_j)`, 20 of shape
`2e_i-e_j-e_k ≡ -(e_i+e_j+e_k)`, 45 of shape `e_i+e_l-e_j-e_k`
(`m1-scripts/types80.py`). This confirms source §5 verbatim.

**(d) Multiplicities.** A nonconstant order-three clock acts freely on the six `B`-rays, so its
permutation is two 3-cycles; each invariant 3-space carries eigenvalues `{1,w,w^2}` (the product
of the cycle's phases is 1), so the spectrum is `(1,1,w,w,w^2,w^2)`, `tr T = 0`, and the `(2,2,2)`
multiplicities follow — as the source claims. QED

## 7. §7's closure claim  [proved]

If exact triples `x_n → x` then every nonzero entry of `P_A(x)` is nonzero for `n` large
(finitely many entries, continuity), so `L_A(x) ⊆ L_A(x_n)` and therefore
`[Lambda_0:L_A(x_n)]` divides `[Lambda_0:L_A(x)]`, i.e. `Delta_A(x_n) | Delta_A(x)`, and
`{3 | Delta_A}` is closed. Hence a proof on a dense subset of the exact triples that produces a
threefold symmetry at every point of the subset covers limits; isolated components still need
separate treatment. Correct as stated.

**(8.4) Two implementation traps in formula (1)  [exact].** Both are neutral for the source,
which states its hypotheses correctly, but both affect re-implementations:

* *Normalisation.* (1) is an identity only when **both** coefficient matrices have every entry of
  modulus exactly `1/sqrt6`. A phase rescaling is harmless (`|c|^4 = 1`), a real rescaling is not:
  if one basis is fed at modulus `sqrt6` instead of `1/sqrt6`, its term is multiplied by `6^4` and
  the support changes. In the `(2,3,3)` triple this gives exactly m5's count: 246 nonzero entries
  with the correct normalisation, **276** with the second basis scaled by 6, at the same lattice
  (`m1-scripts/norm_check.py`). The phase-matrix form
  `P = (1/36)( R(Phi_X) + R(Phi_Y) )`, `R(Phi)_{ij,kl} = sum_r Phi_ir conj(Phi_jr) conj(Phi_kr) Phi_lr`,
  used throughout this audit, is canonical: the `Phi` are unimodular, so the normalisation cannot
  drift.
* *The zero relation.* The position `(i,j) = (k,l)` is always nonzero and yields `r = 0`, which
  imposes no condition and must be dropped before counting rows (or before taking a gcd of minors).
  m5 reports 121 and 79 relation rows at the `(2,3,3)` anchors; dropping the zero relation gives
  **120** and **78**, which is what I compute. The indices and Smith forms are unaffected.
